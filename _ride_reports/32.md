I did "light northern gravel" for the first time this year. It's a good ride. Somehow I managed to remember most of my personal modifications on the route as well. A few nice photos from around Middlesex Fells, which is one of the nicest parts of the ride.

I stopped a few spots for drawing practice -- in the fells, and later at Breakheart. 

Somewhere in Melrose I cam across a sad thing--a bunny rabbit that had been hit by a car and was badly injured but still alive and suffering. I wasn't sure what to do. It was still in the road, but not able to move most of its body. I tried to see if I could nudge it to a safe place but I didn't have much luck; I didn't have anything I could use to safely pick it up. It's tough to be confronted with that kind of situation. Do you just continue on and ignore? Do you try to do something to help, and if so, what? I am no expert on wild rabbits, but it seemed unlikely that the animal was going to make it, badly injured as it was. It crossed my mind that the most compassionate thing might have been to put it out of its misery, but I didn't think I could be the one to do that. I stood nearby a while trying to figure out if there was something I could do or someone I could call -- a wildlife sanctuary maybe. I didn't find much, and after looking around a while and feeling uncertain, I went back over to the rabbit to see that it had passed; head down, eyes glazed. I've been around plenty of death in my human life, but I've never been there at the moment of passing like this. I felt sad that maybe I'd done the wrong thing; I didn't like that I'd prodded the rabbit to try to move it, there in its final moments. Maybe it would have been wiser to see the writing and accept, perhaps try to provide comfort. 

Anyhow, it felt wrong to just leave, even though there was nothing more I could "do". Nearby I found some wildflowers and picked a few to make a little bouquet. I left them by the rabbit in honor and memoriam. Being sad and honoring is a kind of doing.
