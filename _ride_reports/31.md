I haven't gotten out for too many longer rides this season which is unfortunate. But it was a beautiful day and so out I went, leaving on the early side and heading in the direction of my usual Lexington-Concord loop. I haven't ridden it really this year, and so it felt like I ought to check in on the various happenings along the route. 

In the Great Meadow, I decided to sit a while. I sat on a rock and did a little bit of water color of some leaves. I haven't done much drawing or watercolor in a while, but this made me want to do more. It's such a nice way to slow down and be present with something, regardless of the outcome.  

{% include image.html src="/figs/ride_reports/31/img1.jpg" alt="Sketchbook page with watercolor sketches of a tree branch and leaf, with handwritten notes" %}

I was curious about what sort of tree it was that I was painting; with help from AI, the best guess was that it is something from genus [*Malus*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malus), likely a wild crabapple tree. I spotted the early stages of a apple-looking fruit; I'll have to revisit later in the season to see how it grows.

I continued on after a while doing the usual Lexington / Concord loop. Somewhere along the way I had a funny [encounter with a mole](https://www.tiktok.com/@_j117_/video/7661732224231951647?_r=1&_t=ZP-97zrL10tOBj) that I noticed burrowing along beside the trail. 

On the battle road trail, I rode by the sunflower fields. Most are still just green, but a few have started to flower. 

Many other flowers are blooming elsewhere along the ride. 

{% include image-pair.html src1="/figs/ride_reports/31/img4.jpg" alt1="Orange daylily blooming among green foliage" src2="/figs/ride_reports/31/img5.jpg" alt2="Tall spikes of purple loosestrife in the sun" %}

The first of these seems to be an orange daylily. The purple is the invasive loosestrife. I think it would be nice to try collecting flowers along some future rides, either to make a bouquet or maybe to try making some pressed flowers.
