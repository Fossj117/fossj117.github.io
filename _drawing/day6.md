I am starting week 2, which is about mark-making. There is an emphasis on the idea that every line is *expressive* -- it both describes a form/subject, and *expresses* something about the subject; it suggests an opinion, a belief or an emotion. Eviston emphasizes its expressive impact on the viewer, though we might also reflect back on whether a mark expresses what *we* intend, to ourselves. 

A few other key ideas: 

* "How you are making lines says more about you as an artist than what you are drawing." Interesting point. He compares line quality to tone of voice; how you say something is often more important than what you say. 
* A line is "any pathway that the eye can follow".

I practiced mark making with both pen and pencil. 

{% include image.html src="/figs/drawing-day6/mark_making.jpg" alt="two sketchbook pages filled with rows of experimental line and mark-making exercises" %}

I think mark-making is an interesting foundation to focus on in the world of "AI art"--it's like the complete opposite. *What* you are making/rendering is not important -- all the matters is *how* you are making/rendering it, and the tone and expression embedded in those choices. 

In the evening, I did another little drawing practice and reflection in my sketchbook. I wound up thinking more about value here, which is not on the Eviston course arc, but I think I learned a lot. Some of the same principles apply in terms of starting by thinking simply in terms of very light and very dark and adding more complexity. I want to do more practice paying attention to value. 

{% include image.html src="/figs/drawing-day6/coffee_table_still_life.jpg" alt="coffee table still life value practice" %}

I also think it's the case that it's a fair bit trickier to figure out value with hatching and pen only. I think I want to try focusing more on using just wash to get that right; I should go back to the Dunn book on shading at some point though.
