Learning about vanishing points and perspective. Some things to think about: 

* Perspective as like a rendering choice is something I don't fully understand conceptually; Eviston's framing is like "the more vanishing points you use, the more believable/realistic it will look". 
* Eye-level / horizon line as a key calibration. 
* When drawing the face that is in perspective, how to choose length? Also how far away should vanishing points be on the horizon? 
  * This part seems to be left "intuitive" in a lot of drawing classes? But I like to understand the underlying principles. 
  * One principle: sides that points more directly away from you appear more foreshortened
* I found a reference here that seems to have a lot more technical detail which I think will be useful for me to work through: 
  * [Perspective in the world](https://www.handprint.com/HP/WCL/perspect1.html): Foundations of linear perspective, framed around the viewer rather than the object: distance cues (parallax, occlusion, texture gradients), the four perspective facts, the perspective setup, and common perspective distortions.
  * [Central perspective](https://www.handprint.com/HP/WCL/perspect2.html): One-point perspective in depth, including the visual ray method, orthogonals and central recession, perspective gradients, distance and size scaling, constructing a 1PP cube, and early Renaissance methods.
  * [Two point perspective](https://www.handprint.com/HP/WCL/perspect3.html): Covers 2PP construction: rotating the vanishing points, locating measure points, constructing a 2PP cube, inclined lines and planes, and practical questions like how far apart to space the vanishing points for a given object.
  * [Three point perspective](https://www.handprint.com/HP/WCL/perspect4.html): Two methods for constructing 3PP drawings (the perspective sketch method and the horizon line method), with worked examples of constructing a 3PP cube.
  * [Advanced perspective techniques](https://www.handprint.com/HP/WCL/perspect5.html): Perspective for complex plane figures and solid forms, drawing buildings from blueprints or plans, plus paraline (axonometric) and curvilinear perspective systems.
  * [Shadows, reflections & atmosphere](https://www.handprint.com/HP/WCL/perspect6.html): The perspective of cast shadows (from the sun and from local lights), reflections, aerial perspective, and rainbows.

Here's a ChatGPT supported set of additional references to dive deeper into later on: 

* [Bruce MacEvoy — "Two-Point Perspective"](https://handprint.com/HP/WCL/perspect3.html): The most detailed explanation of the geometry behind measuring points, station points, viewing distance, and constructing exact cubes in 2PP. Advanced and fairly dense.
* [City Tech — "Two-Point Perspective"](https://openlab.citytech.cuny.edu/woolley-comd1103fa17/files/2017/11/Chapter-5-Two-Point-Perspective.pdf): Clear, diagram-heavy walkthrough of the complete construction, including the station point, center of vision, vanishing points, and measuring points.
* [BYU–Idaho — "Measuring Points II"](https://courses.byui.edu/art110_new/art110/week02/measuring_points2.html): A practical, step-by-step exercise in constructing a precise cube in two-point perspective using measuring points.
* [Rex Vicat Cole — *Perspective for Artists*](https://vintageinkwell.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Perspective-for-Artists-by-Rex-Vicat-Cole.pdf): A longer, artist-oriented discussion of perspective, depth measurement, station points, diagonals, and mechanical construction. Older language and terminology.
* [Ernest Norling — *Perspective Made Easy*](https://archive.org/download/perspective-made-easy/perspective-made-easy_text.pdf): A gradual introduction to perspective drawing as a whole, including two-point perspective, divisions, diagonals, and mechanical perspective. Best as a general introductory reference.
